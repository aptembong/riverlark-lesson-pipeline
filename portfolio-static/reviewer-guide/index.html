Your queue at a glance

The automated checks sort every passage before you see it. The lane tells you how closely to read. It never makes the decision for you.

Green

Spot-check

About 30 seconds · 10% random sample

Every check passed. A random 10% of greens come to you. Skim the passage, read the highlighted examples, and run the rubric. If anything feels off, pull it into a full review.

Yellow

Full review

About 3 minutes · every yellow

A fit issue, or one or two yellow-list words. Read the whole passage, the check results and the full rubric, then choose a decision.

Red

Not in your queue

Redrafted automatically

A red-list word, three or more yellow-list words, a broken schema, or a misquoted example. It's redrafted and checked again. You see reds only in the weekly send-back review.

Script

Lesson script review

About 5 minutes · every lesson

After a passage is approved, you review its lesson script before video. See the video stage for what a finished script looks like.

Times are planning estimates from the quality plan, used in the case study's editor-hours calculator.

How the week runs

Work oldest yellow first

Yellows hold up a batch. Clear them before spot-checks, oldest first, so no lesson waits more than two working days.

Stop at the time cap

About 12 editor hours a week across three editors during production. When you reach your share, stop and tell the Managing Editor what's left. Tired reading misses things.

Calibrate before production

All three editors decide the same 10 sample passages on their own, then compare. Talk through every disagreement before the first batch. Repeat when the prompt changes.

Reading the automated results

Each passage arrives with six check results. Here's what each one measures and what to do when it misses. The rules match pipeline/validate.py and are fixed before a batch runs.

CheckWhat it measuresWhat a miss usually meansWhat you do
SchemaEvery field the lesson needs is there, including the four lesson hooks.The draft is broken. It goes red and is redrafted, so you won't see it.Nothing. If one ever reaches you, report a wrong lane.
LengthWord count, against the grade's target range plus or minus 10 words.The passage is too thin to carry the skill, or too long for the grade.Close to range and still teaching well: approve with notes. Far off: regenerate.
Average sentenceWords per sentence, against the grade's range plus or minus 2. Not checked for poems.Sentences are too long for the grade, which makes the text harder than its words.Name the paragraphs with the longest sentences in your note. Regenerate if most of it runs long.
Skill evidenceHow many skill examples the passage carries, and whether each one is quoted exactly.Too few examples (yellow), or a quote that isn't in the passage word for word (red).The script counts examples. You decide whether each one actually shows the skill.
Readability estimateA free Flesch-Kincaid grade estimate, against the grade's expected range.Words or sentences sit above or below the grade. It's an estimate and only routes the passage.Run the licensed Lexile check. Lexile decides. If they disagree, log it.
Representation flagsWords on the red and yellow lists (deficit framing, stereotypes, ableist language, savior stories).A word that often signals a problem. Red-list words, or three or more yellow-list words, send it red.Read the whole sentence. A list can only match words. Whether the story treats people well is your call.
Lexile decides readability. The Flesch-Kincaid number only routes passages. Riverlark's licensed Lexile analyzer runs before you approve, against the brief's Lexile range. If the estimate and Lexile disagree, Lexile wins, and you log the gap so the team can track it by grade band.

The rubric

Seven checks for every passage you open. On a full review, also score teaching quality from 1 to 5. Checks 4 to 7 come from the representation framework and need a person every time.

The skill is taught, not just mentioned
Clear enough for the grade
Fits the grade's length and reading level
The main character solves their own problem
Culture, family and place read as everyday life
No stereotypes or deficit language
Disability, if present, uses person-first or neutral words
Teaching quality, 1 to 55 means a teacher could use it tomorrow as is. 3 means it teaches the skill but needs your notes. 1 means it doesn't teach the skill.
Where these come fromThe representation framework on the case study's Quality and governance tab. The same rules are written into the prompt.

When to overrule the lane

You always can. A lane is the checks' best guess. When yours is different, make your call and log the wrong lane, so the rules get better instead of you working around them.

Green, but…

Pull it into a full review

Every check passed, but an example is quoted exactly and still doesn't show the skill, or the character is rescued by someone else. Checks can't see either one.

Yellow, but fine

Approve it

The estimate missed but Lexile is in range, or the passage is a few words over and reads well. Approve and log that the check disagreed with you.

Flag, but fair

Approve and report the list

A flagged word is used fairly in context. This happened for real: an early validator flagged “authentic” near “culture,” the exact language the framework asks for, so the pattern was removed and the reason recorded in the code.

Never change a check yourself. Validator rules change only through the Managing Editor, and only between batches, so a rule is never tuned to the batch it scores. Your wrong-lane reports are the evidence for the next change.

Report a wrong lane

Fill this in, copy the line, and paste it into the review log. It takes under a minute. The AI Workflow team reads the log every week.
