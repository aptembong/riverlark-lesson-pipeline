The pipeline in one minute

One brief goes in, one reviewed video lesson comes out. Claude drafts, scripts check, and people make every decision.

STAGE 1

Data layer

Each TEKS standard becomes a brief with its grade's targets.

STAGE 2

AI generation

Claude drafts the passage and quotes its skill examples.

STAGE 3

Automated checks

Six checks sort the draft green, yellow or red.

STAGE 4

Editor review

Editors decide every yellow and a 10% sample of green.

STAGE 5

Lesson script

The approved passage becomes a gradual-release script. Editors review it.

STAGE 6

Video build and QA

A vendor renders the video. Automated checks, then a 10% spot-check.

STAGE 7

Deploy and improve

Publish by TEKS code. Send-back reasons feed the next prompt change.

Where you come in

Writers

You make the fixes and shape the scripts

  • Revise a passage after an editor's “Approve with notes,” then send it back through the checks.
  • Tighten lesson scripts in Stage 5 so the I do, We do and You do steps teach the same skill.
  • Tell the Managing Editor when the same fix keeps coming up. That's how prompt changes start.
Editors

You decide

  • Review every yellow and a random 10% of greens, using the rubric.
  • Approve, approve with notes, regenerate, or reject. Every decision but Approve needs a note.
  • Review every lesson script before video.

Fixing a yellow

Most yellows are routine. Fix the cause, not the number.

The check saysUsually becauseFix it byDon't
Too shortThe passage ends before the skill has roomAdding a paragraph that carries another skill examplePad with description
Too longExtra scenes or a second ideaCutting the part that doesn't serve the skillCut the examples
Sentences too longIdeas joined with “and,” “but,” “because”Splitting at the joinsMake choppy fragments
Reads too hardRare words or long sentencesSwapping in grade-level words, then shorter sentencesSimplify the skill itself
Reads too easy (often grades 9-12)Plain words throughoutChoosing more precise words for key ideasLengthen sentences past the cap
Too few examplesThe skill is named but not shown enoughWriting one more moment that shows itQuote the same sentence twice
Quote not foundThe example was paraphrasedCopying the sentence from the passage exactlyEdit the passage to match the quote
Flagged wordA word that often signals deficit framing or a stereotypeRewriting the phrase in asset-based languageSwap in a synonym that means the same thing

Four rules

Checks sort, people decide

A lane is a guess about how closely to read. An editor makes every call.

Quotes are exact

Skill examples are copied word for word, so the script and video highlight the right sentence.

Lexile decides

The free estimate only routes. The licensed analyzer is the readability gate.

Every edit is rechecked

After any fix, the passage runs through the checks again before approval.

Part 1 · about 5 minutes

Lane drill

Read each set of check results and pick the lane the validator would give. Four come straight from the sample run. Three are training copies with one change each, so you can see what sends a passage red.

The lane rules

  • Green: every check passed.
  • Yellow: any fit issue (length, sentence length, example count, readability), or one or two yellow-list words.
  • Red: any red-list word, three or more yellow-list words, a broken schema, or an example that isn't quoted exactly.

Part 2 · about 15 minutes

Five real passages

These are v2.1 passages from the November sample run, with the validator's real results. First call the lane. Then review it as an editor and compare your decision with an editor's.

The editor decisions here are illustrative, written to show how the rubric applies. In calibration, editors often differ on Approve versus Approve with notes, and that's the conversation worth having.

Your results
